# Follow-up templates

Use these when someone hasn't replied, or when you're circling back after acting on advice someone gave you. Both moves matter more than most people think. Most job seekers send one message and stop. A second touch on its own puts you ahead of most outreach a busy person receives.

A simple cadence works well: send a first follow-up around 3 business days after no response, a second around 7 business days after that, then let it go. If you had an actual conversation, send the thank-you within 24-48 hours instead of waiting on this cadence.

## 1. First follow-up, no response (around day 3)

```
Hi [Name], following up in case my last note got buried. No worries if now
isn't a good time. If it's useful, happy to send over a specific question
instead of asking for a full call.
```

## 2. Second follow-up, still no response (around day 7)

```
Hi [Name], one more try, totally understand if this isn't the right moment.
If you ever have 10 minutes down the road I'd still welcome the chance to
hear your take on [specific topic]. Either way, I hope things are going well
at [Company].
```

After this, stop. Move your energy to the next person on your list.

## 3. Reporting back after you acted on their advice

```
Hi [Name], wanted to close the loop. I took your suggestion to [specific
action they recommended] and [what happened as a result]. Really appreciated
the steer, and if anything changes on your end that you think I should know
about, I'd love to stay in touch.
```

This message does double duty. It's considerate on its own, and it reopens a door that a one-off thank-you note closes.

## 4. Checking in a few weeks after a referral or introduction

```
Hi [Name], quick update. Things are moving along with [Company/role] after
your introduction to [Contact], really appreciate you making that happen.
Will let you know how it goes either way.
```
