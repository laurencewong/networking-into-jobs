# Sources

All sources below are free, public content (videos, blog posts, or institutional web guides). No paid courses or paywalled material was used. Dates are the publish date where one could be confirmed; "undated" means no publish date was found on the page itself. Where a number or quote is attributed to a source, it reflects that source's own claim, not something independently verified against a third-party dataset unless noted.

## Steve Dalton

Author of *The 2-Hour Job Search*; career director at Duke University's Fuqua School of Business. Creator of the LAMP method (target-list building) and the 3B7 Routine (follow-up cadence).

1. **"LAMP List Video"** (video, re-hosted on YouTube, originally 2018-07-20)
   https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_bJeVDNF6-o
   Dalton's own walkthrough of building a scored list of roughly 40 target employers in about 70 minutes, sourced from admired companies, an alumni database, active job postings, and companies mentioned in industry news, then sorted by a gut-feel motivation score.

2. **"Tracking Job Search Outreach: the 3B7 Routine"** (video, Duke Fuqua's official channel, 2019-06-27)
   https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KeVvvHB1lUA
   Introduces his named follow-up system: set a reminder at 3 business days (move on to the next contact in parallel) and another at 7 business days (a quiet second touch).

3. **"Why not the '1-Hour Job Search'?"** (article, 2hourjobsearch.com, 2012-08-05)
   https://2hourjobsearch.com/articles/why-not-the-1-hour-job-search
   Clarifies that "2 hours" is front-loaded strategy time, not a daily quota, and describes the first outreach batch: a top-5 shortlist from the larger target list, each sent an initial message, then followed up on the 3B7 cadence.

4. **"From the Forest City: A Better Way to Job Search with Steve Dalton"** (video interview, 2020-09-17)
   https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WidMo7NMQ4w
   An interview where Dalton frames his method as Prioritize, Contact, Convince, and describes the "Harvest Cycle," a process for converting an informational conversation into an actual interview, added to the book's second edition.

## J.T. O'Donnell / Work It Daily

Career coach; founder of the coaching platform Work It Daily.

5. **"How To Use Informational Interviews In Job Search"** (video, Work It Daily YouTube channel, 2025-02-12)
   https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cpQjccArNGs
   Her fullest process: build a target-company "bucket list," identify 5 to 10 contacts per company, open with one easy question, request a short (10 to 20 minute) informational interview explicitly framed as not a job ask, and close by requesting one more introduction.

6. **"How to Ask for Job Referrals Like a Pro"** (video, Work It Daily YouTube channel, 2024-09-06)
   https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kEVfmzc7Omg
   A short, single-technique video: when reconnecting with a contact about a role you've applied to, ask for their advice on standing out with the hiring manager rather than asking directly for a referral.

7. **"How To Set Up An Informational Interview"** (article, workitdaily.com, undated)
   https://www.workitdaily.com/c/blog/set-up-an-informational-interview
   A written three-step version of the same flow: narrow to 2 to 3 target career paths, send a personalized (not generic) LinkedIn connection note, then ask for a short call once you've exchanged a few messages.

## Austin Belcak / Cultivated Culture

Career coach; founder of the career site Cultivated Culture.

8. **"How To Network: 4+ Real Strategies For Connecting The Right Way"** (article, cultivatedculture.com, 2020-11-16)
   https://cultivatedculture.com/how-to-network/
   His core philosophy piece: target people with direct influence over your desired role, not generic events, and build the relationship through his "value equation" (give before you ask) before any referral ask.

9. **"How To Write A Cold Email That Gets A Response"** (article, cultivatedculture.com, 2021-05-24)
   https://cultivatedculture.com/cold-email/
   A tactical cold-email structure: a specific subject line, a "why you're reaching out" opener, proof you've already tried to solve the problem yourself, and a simple binary question so the reply takes one line.

10. **"Informational Interview Questions: How To Ace Your Next One"** (article, cultivatedculture.com, 2021-02-24)
    https://cultivatedculture.com/questions-to-ask-in-informational-interview/
    Logistics and a question bank for the call itself: find and verify the contact's email, cap the meeting at 30 minutes, and work through roughly 10 questions covering career path, day-to-day, and challenges.

## Madeline Mann / Self Made Millennial

Former in-house recruiter; founder of the YouTube channel Self Made Millennial.

11. **"Best Way to Connect on LinkedIn - 5 HIGH IMPACT Invitation Message Samples!"** (video, 2019-10-31)
    https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lHQlcsuAl_c
    Five concrete LinkedIn connection-request openers (the compliment, continuing an existing exchange, finding something in common, a shared connection, offering something concrete), each with a sample line.

12. **"How to Land an Informational Interview and the Best Questions to Ask!"** (video, 2020-12-17)
    https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BdC5-q2Fc0U
    A four-step call structure (introduce yourself and your goal, ask about the role, ask about their path, ask for advice) and an explicit instruction to send the thank-you within 24 hours.

13. **"Coronavirus Job Search: How to Network Online for a Job"** (video, 2020-04-09)
    https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Wq5RqrDOgHE
    Splits outreach into warm contacts (a simple "it's been a while" reconnection) and cold contacts (comment on their content first, then send a "this-or-that" binary advice question); introduces her "60 seconds of value" idea, doing something small and unsolicited for a contact before any ask.

## Phyl Terry / Never Search Alone

Author of *Never Search Alone*; founder of Collaborative Gain and the Job Search Council model.

14. **neversearchalone.org** (website, undated, accessed 2026-10-29)
    https://www.neversearchalone.org/
    Describes his "Job Search Council" model: small peer groups of job seekers moving through a structured search together, built around three stages he calls Candidate-Market Fit, networking and outreach, and interview and negotiation ("Play to Win-Win").

15. **"Rethinking The Job Search | Q&A with Phyl Terry from Never Search Alone"** (video, NUS Business School, upload dated 2026-03-16)
    https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vu7jISFpiBw
    Terry explains his "listening tour" step done before any networking or applying: asking people who already know your work what role they'd point you toward, meant to counter three common mistakes (applying before you know what you want, generic positioning, and locking into an untested path).

16. **Lenny's Podcast, "Land your dream job in today's market" ft. Phyl Terry** (podcast interview with show notes, 2024-09-12)
    https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/land-your-dream-job-phyl-terry
    Terry, interviewed by Lenny Rachitsky, walks through Job Search Councils, three types of "listening tour" conversations (ex-colleagues, broader network, recruiters), and negotiation framing that discusses company priorities before salary. Sourced from the episode's show notes rather than a full transcript; treat any direct quotes from this episode as unverified against the raw audio.

## Jeff Su

YouTube creator covering career and productivity tactics; former Google product manager.

17. **"Reach out to Recruiters on LinkedIn (the right way!)"** (video, 2020-09-16)
    https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jnzh5QTKbsw
    A sequence for cold recruiter outreach: connect without mentioning the role, then ask to be connected to someone in the role itself for a casual conversation, then follow up with the recruiter afterward citing something specific you learned.

18. **"LinkedIn: How to Connect Like a Pro"** (video, 2020-09-09)
    https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9BdbGZtnFnQ
    Applies Mark Granovetter's "strength of weak ties" research to job search and gives three LinkedIn tactics for finding and reaching weak-tie contacts: a school or company's alumni search filter, direct messaging inside relevant LinkedIn groups, and a three-part message structure (relate, compliment, add value). Cites an 84% statistic on weak ties; this guide did not independently verify that figure against the original research.

19. **"Informational Interviews: Best Questions to Ask"** (video, 2020-10-14)
    https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4b2iagdHw8M
    Organizes informational-interview questions into three categories (role and tasks, career path, industry knowledge) and recommends always closing with a request for one more introduction.

## Lenny Rachitsky / Lenny's Newsletter

Former Airbnb product manager; writes Lenny's Newsletter and hosts Lenny's Podcast, both focused on product management careers.

20. **"How to break into product management"** (article, lennysnewsletter.com, 2019-06-14)
    https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-to-get-into-product-management
    Describes four paths into a first PM role (internal transfer, formal junior-PM programs, joining a startup, or starting a company) and notes that the startup path in particular runs on having a founder connection, alongside a list of core skills to demonstrate while networking in.

(See source 16 above for his interview with Phyl Terry, also credited under Rachitsky's byline as interviewer.)

## University career centers

Independent guides from three university career-services offices, included because their informational-interview scripts and etiquette rules converge closely with each other and with the individual coaches above, despite being written separately.

21. **Harvard FAS Career Services, "Harvard College Guide to Making Connections"** (guide, undated)
    https://careerservices.fas.harvard.edu/resources/harvard-college-guide-to-making-connections-2/
    A full-lifecycle guide covering target-list building through an alumni database of 3,500+ advisors, outreach scripts, interview etiquette, and a specific closing line for the referral ask. Recommends a one-week follow-up window and giving up after two unanswered follow-ups.

22. **Stanford Career Education, "The Informational Interview Guide"** (guide, undated)
    https://careered.stanford.edu/resources
    The most detailed of the three: full sample outreach templates (including a distinct template for asking someone to make an introduction, and another for when you're already referred by someone), a five-step call structure, and a 24 to 48 hour thank-you window.

23. **UC Berkeley Career Engagement, "Informational Interviews" and "Asking for a Job Referral"** (two linked guides, undated)
    https://career.berkeley.edu/start-exploring/informational-interviews/ and https://career.berkeley.edu/prepare-for-success/networking/asking-for-a-job-referral/
    Berkeley is the one source here that treats the referral ask as its own distinct step rather than folding it into general interview advice, and frames it with the image of asking for directions rather than asking for a ride.

## Notes on verification

A small number of details surfaced in research were flagged by the researchers as unconfirmed and were deliberately left out of this guide or stated with a caveat where included:
- A specific "Mnookin two-pager" exercise mentioned in passing in the Lenny Rachitsky/Phyl Terry episode was not detailed enough in available show notes to describe accurately.
- A short clip attributed to J.T. O'Donnell on "Creating a Connection Story" could not be fully confirmed as her own voice versus another Work It Daily coach, so it was left out of the main guide.
- Exact response-rate percentages cited by Belcak (80%, 40%) and Su (84%) are each source's own stated figures, not numbers this guide's research independently traced to primary data.
